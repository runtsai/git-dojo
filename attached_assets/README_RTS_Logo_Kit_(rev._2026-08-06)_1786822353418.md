## Two things that will bite you if nobody says them out loud

**The transparent master will disappear on a white background.** The letterforms are ivory. Post the master PNG straight to LinkedIn and the platform composites transparency against its own surface — which in light mode is near-white, and ivory on near-white is close to invisible. This is the single most likely way to accidentally publish a blank-looking logo. Use the flattened midnight version for anything you post, and keep the transparent master for cases where you are placing it onto a background yourself.

**Circular crops eat wide marks.** RTS is a 2.5-to-1 horizontal mark, and LinkedIn renders personal photos as circles. Drop the wide mark into a square and the R and the S get sliced off at the sides. The avatar and company-logo files here already hold the mark inside the inscribed circle, so they survive a round crop with room to spare. Use those rather than cropping the wide version yourself.

## Which files carry ™

The avatars, the company logo and the favicon do not. At those sizes a ™ renders as an illegible speck and adds nothing; the mark is doing the work.

The covers, the banner and the share card do. They are large enough to carry it legibly, and they are the placements a person actually reads.

The specimen does not, because the specimen is the specimen — it is a record of what was in use on July 24, 2026, and it does not get edited to suit a later preference.

## Using the mark

Never ®. ™ only, until a registration is actually granted.

Do not recolor the letterforms to anything but ivory or midnight. Do not add a stroke, shadow, glow, gradient or outline. Do not stretch, condense, rotate, or set it at an angle. Do not place it on a busy photograph. Do not rebuild it in a typeface that looks close.

Give it clear space on all four sides equal to the height of the letters. Do not reproduce it smaller than about 90px wide on screen; below that the cut corners stop resolving and it reads as noise.
